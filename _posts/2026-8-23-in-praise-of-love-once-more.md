title:  "In Praise of Love, Once More"
date:   2026-8-23
---

The modern condition is defined by a pervasive spiritual exhaustion, largely born from the total victory of instrumental reason. Under the logic of global capital, every dimension of human life is reduced to exchange value, risk management, and utilitarian calculus. Even the most intimate human experiences—courtship, domesticity, and devotion—are increasingly framed through the sterile language of emotional return on investment and self-optimization. Yet, while radical materialism has long diagnosed this alienation, its proposed remedies have consistently accelerated the very sickness they set out to cure.

The fundamental flaw of secular radical critique lies in its inability to ground resistance in anything transcendent. In *In Praise of Love*, Alain Badiou perceptively attacks the modern commodification of romance, contrasting algorithmic "love without risk" with love as a disruptive, transformative Event. Slavoj Žižek similarly identifies authentic love as an uncalculated, traumatic rupture with market rationalism. Yet, because these thinkers remain bound to strict dialectical materialism, love remains an accidental anomaly—a fleeting rupture within a closed, immanent universe, stripped of any metaphysical horizon or enduring sacred sanctuary.

This materialist blind spot becomes fatal in its treatment of the family. From Friedrich Engels to contemporary anti-natalist and abolitionist theorists, radical thought has historically treated the domestic unit as little more than an ideological apparatus designed for the reproduction of labor power and bourgeois property. In waging war on the family to dismantle capitalism, radical theory discarded the primary organic vessel capable of resisting total commodification. A parent sitting beside a sick child through the night or an individual enduring hardship for the sake of a partner does not act on economic incentives. Unconditional love and self-sacrifice shatter the logic of utility entirely: they represent expenditure without demand for return, proving that human meaning is found not in self-preservation, but in devotion to a reality beyond the solitary ego.

While formal religion has receded from the modern consciousness, viewed by secular sensibilities as an obsolete artifact of the past, the family remains the final intuitive sanctuary of the sacred. It is the only universal bastion left with the power to actively combat capitalism, precisely because it requires no abstract dogma to be felt—its truth is lived. Almost every human being instinctively understands the gravity of holding a newborn or standing beside a dying elder. In a world that commands the monetization of every waking hour, the familial bond stands as our most potent weapon of resistance, preserving the visceral knowledge that the greatest human acts are rooted not in calculation, but in absolute, unconditional devotion.

Even Antonio Gramsci’s sophisticated turn toward cultural hegemony reveals this instrumental dead end. Gramsci understood that power is sustained through the lived fabric of culture, common sense, and organic traditions. Yet his ambition was not to sanctify culture as the repository of human spirit, but to weaponize it as an ideological battleground for counter-hegemony. When culture, faith, and kinship are reduced to mere instruments of political warfare, they lose the transcendent depth that makes human life worth living.

The tragic irony of secular radicalism is that by dissolving the organic bonds of family, lineage, and spiritual reverence in the name of abstract solidarity, it paved the way for absolute atomization. One cannot love an abstract collective if one has never learned the weight of particular devotion. Stripped of the sacred armor of the household and living tradition, the isolated individual stands naked before the marketplace and the state. True solidarity cannot be engineered through economic restructuring or ideological warfare; it is forged at the hearth, where the beauty of unconditional sacrifice reveals that the human soul was never meant to be bought, sold, or calculated.
