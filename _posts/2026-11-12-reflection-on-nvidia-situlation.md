Since the Nvidia scandal broke, the loudest chorus across finance feeds has been the same predictable mantra: *"The fundamentals still work!"* It is the classic midwit investor reflex—clinging to trailing earnings reports while completely missing the destruction of what actually made the stock go up in the first place: symbolic power.

The truth is that a company’s profit and its stock price are mechanically disconnected. There is no invisible lever inside the market that automatically cranks a share price higher whenever net income rises. The actual chain of events is purely social and psychological: a company generates profit, investors observe it, and because those investors have been conditioned to believe that profit equals value, they buy the stock—and that buying pressure is what moves the price. 

Most investors miss this nuance, and that single blind spot compounds into an entire delusion. They confuse a learned social catechism with a law of physics. Because they think a magic lever connects accounting to valuation, they look at the market as it "should" behave in a textbook rather than as it actually is—a liquidity and belief machine governed by narrative velocity, attention, and myth.

For years, Nvidia wasn't priced like a hardware vendor; it was priced like a sovereign religion. Jensen Huang, with his black leather jacket and unbroken founder aura, was the human totem absorbing all of the market’s metaphysical faith in artificial intelligence. That mystique was the engine behind the multiple. Without that symbolic power—or with that aura fractured by scandal and the threat of replacement by a safe, risk-averse corporate caretaker—Nvidia risks being re-rated from a mythic monopoly down to a mere industrial utility. Utilities can print massive profits every quarter while their stocks go completely sideways.

When you fail to see that price is a semiotic phenomenon rather than an accounting equation, every decision gets distorted. You wait for the spreadsheet to confirm the story, meaning you buy late after the narrative is already consensus. When the aura breaks and the smart money exits, you see trailing earnings rising against a falling stock price, mistake a collapsing multiple for a "cheap bargain," and hold all the way down waiting for a magic referee to restore fair value.

Until Nvidia finds a new leader with the sheer charismatic gravity to re-enchant the market, all the quarterly earnings beats in the world won't bring back the spell.
